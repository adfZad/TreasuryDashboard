Executive Summary: The organization currently prepares its Treasury Report and Dashboard primarily through Microsoft Excel. Information is collected from multiple:

Business Units

Bank statements

Financing schedules

Working capital facilities

Loan schedules

Investment information

Cash flow forecasts

Other treasury-related sources

The existing Excel-based process involves manual data collection, consolidation, validation, commentary, review, and approval activities. This creates dependency on spreadsheets, increases the possibility of data inconsistencies, makes historical reporting difficult to reproduce, and limits visibility into status of submissions and approvals.

The organization requires a centralized web-based Treasury Reporting and Dashboard Web App Portal.

The proposed application will allow BUs to enter or upload their data into a controlled system. Submitted data will pass through a structured review and approval workflow involving BU Finance Heads, Business Heads, Corporate Finance, and the Chief Financial Officer.

Only CFO-approved data will be published to final Treasury Dashboard for viewing by the CEO and Board Members.

This system will be hosted on Microsoft Internet Information Services, IIS, and Microsoft SQL Server will be used as the primary application database.

The objective is to establish a secure, auditable, database-driven Treasury reporting platform that becomes single source of truth for Treasury reporting.

Conclusion: Proposed Treasury Reporting and Dashboard Automation System will replace current spreadsheet-centric reporting process with a centralized, controlled, and auditable Treasury reporting platform.

Business Units will remain responsible for entering Treasury information, while a formal multi-level review and approval workflow will establish accountability and governance.

The key process will be: 

BU Data Submission → BU Finance Head Review → Business Head Review → Corporate Finance Review → CFO Approval → Approved Treasury Snapshot → CEO and Board Dashboard

Microsoft SQL Server shall become the primary source of Treasury reporting data, while the web application hosted on IIS shall provide users with secure data entry, review, approval, reporting, and dashboard capabilities.

The existing Treasury Excel workbook will remain an important functional reference during detailed system design, but spreadsheet cell structures and dependencies will not be carried directly into the new application architecture.

The target outcome is a reliable Treasury reporting platform that improves data accuracy, reporting speed, governance, accountability, historical traceability, and executive visibility.
